# Example study — first-time recording and recovery

**Illustrative plan only. No human sessions or findings have been performed.**

Question: Can first-time desktop users select a microphone, recognize when capture is active, stop capture and recover from permission denial?

Method: Formative moderated study, one platform/build per round, representative first-time users. Do not assume 5 participants are statistically representative.

Tasks:

1. “You're about to dictate a short note. Get RimV ready to use your headset microphone.”
2. “Start the note. Tell me when you think RimV is capturing your voice.”
3. “Stop capturing now, and copy the final text.”
4. After resetting the environment with consent, simulate denied microphone access: “Try to make RimV ready again.”

Pilot using synthetic speech only. Keep permission reset technically safe and repeatable. For each task, note first action, state interpretation, completion, help, unexpected transitions and recovery; do not infer hesitation as confusion without other evidence.

Potential **hypotheses, not findings**:

- The live state may be too subtle.
- The stop control may not be prominent enough.
- Permission recovery might depend on hidden settings.

If actual sessions substantiate any hypothesis, create a coded finding and define a post-fix retest. If a participant cannot complete a safety-critical stop action, escalate promptly to the release owner without waiting for a larger sample.
