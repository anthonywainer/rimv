# Running a moderated session

## Roles

**Moderator:** consent, task presentation, neutral prompts, safety, follow-up. **Observer/notetaker:** time-coded behavior, visible UI state and assistance; does not coach. **Participant:** performs realistic tasks using their usual interaction method. One person may moderate and take notes for a small study, but distinguish notes from assumptions.

## Suggested session (adjust to participant and research)

- 5 min: welcome, consent, environment/device check.
- 5 min: brief context and optional think-aloud demonstration.
- 20–35 min: selected tasks, one at a time.
- 5–10 min: post-task questions and debrief.

Reserve extra time for assistive-technology setup or fatigue breaks; do not demand think-aloud when it interferes with the participant's normal interaction.

## Moderator opening script

“Thank you for helping us evaluate RimV. We're testing the interface, not you. There are no wrong answers. Please work as you naturally would. If you're comfortable, tell me what you're expecting or looking for. You may skip any question, stop a task or end the session at any time. We'll only record what you agreed to.”

Confirm recording permission separately; app microphone permission is **not** participant consent to research recording.

## Neutral facilitation

Allowed: “What are you looking for?”, “What do you expect will happen?”, “What made you choose that?”, “What would you try next?”

Avoid: “Wouldn't the green button be clearer?”, “Notice the Download Model option”, “You should click Settings.” Do not praise particular choices or rescue participants at the first hesitation. If help is necessary for wellbeing, accessibility or progression, provide it and mark the task **assisted**.

## During each task

1. Read only the task scenario.
2. Record start state and environment.
3. Observe first action, pauses, corrections, wrong turns, error messages and whether the participant notices status.
4. Ask neutral clarifications sparingly; capture exact short quotes only with permission.
5. Apply the predefined end/assistance rule.
6. Ask one or two short post-task questions: “What was difficult or unexpected?” “What would you change?”

Do not infer inner thoughts from facial expression, elapsed time or click behavior alone. If a participant says nothing, write what happened, not what they “felt.”

## Interruptions and failures

Separate failures caused by product, test environment, task wording, network/audio fixture and facilitator intervention. A technical crash may end the task and still create a valid observation; note exactly what happened and what could not be evaluated.

## End

Ask for final feedback and perceived privacy expectations, thank the participant, repeat contact/deletion options, and secure session notes. Do not tell participants a particular design is proven successful based on one session.
