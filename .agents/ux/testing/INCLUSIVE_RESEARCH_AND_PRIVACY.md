# Inclusive research and participant privacy

## Recruitment and participation

Offer a realistic way for participants to request accommodations. Where the product supports the use case, recruit across relevant interaction modes and devices: keyboard, screen reader, magnification, touch, speech alternatives, high contrast and large text. Let participants use familiar assistive technology instead of forcing unfamiliar tools.

Schedule breaks and allow task skipping. Do not assume one participant represents every person with a disability. Respect participant terminology and preferences. Recruit through appropriate, consensual channels.

## Accessible sessions

- Share accessible task wording ahead of time when it will not invalidate the study.
- Provide a compatible remote platform or accessible in-person setup.
- Allow participant-selected screen reader and input settings.
- Adjust the moderator's technique; think-aloud may be impractical while listening to a screen reader.
- Evaluate the **actual participant experience** and run technical accessibility checks separately.

User testing is not a WCAG conformance audit. For formal accessibility evaluation, use the Part 4C.2 procedures and W3C WCAG-EM guidance.

## Consent and data minimization

Before the session, explain purpose, tasks, duration, voluntary nature, recording options, who can access materials, retention/deletion plan and withdrawal procedure. Obtain consent appropriate to your setting; do not assume a generic template meets local legal requirements. If real participants are involved, obtain any needed legal/privacy review.

Prefer synthetic transcripts and prerecorded non-sensitive audio. Avoid real user histories, actual contacts, account credentials and private documents. Never require participants to speak sensitive personal material. Confirm screen and system notifications are sanitized before any screen recording. Do not automatically enable app telemetry, audio recordings or screen captures because the session is a test.

## Data handling

- Give participants coded IDs such as `P01`, never put names in shared research logs.
- Separate contact/consent records from behavior notes.
- Store raw audio/video, if collected, only in an approved access-controlled location with a defined retention period.
- Do not commit raw research recordings, transcripts, consent forms or identifying notes to Git.
- Do not paste identifiable participant data into AI tools without authorization and suitable controls.
- Use minimal evidence in issue reports; cropped/redacted screenshots where sufficient.
- Honor consent limits and deletion requests under the established study procedure.

## Remote studies

Check microphone/audio routing, screen sharing, lag and privacy before starting the first task. Document if delay materially changes the experience. Provide a way to stop sharing or exit without embarrassment. If remote setup fails, mark that session/task incomplete rather than treating it as a product failure.
